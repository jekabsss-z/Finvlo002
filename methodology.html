THE FINVLO METHODQuality analytics, not investment advice.

METHODOLOGY / TRANSPARENT BY DESIGN

One score. A visible method.

We measure the quality of a business. Price does not enter the Quality Score.

01

Rank each metric

Rank a company against sector peers, or the whole universe, on a 0–100 scale. Flip metrics where lower is better.

02

Build the pillars

Weight metric scores into a quality pillar such as Profitability or Balance sheet.

03

Combine the score

Weight pillars into one 0–100 Quality Score. No price multiple is included.

04

Read the checklist

Where a threshold applies, show pass or fail and the number of checks passed.

THE COMPONENTS

Every metric, in plain language.

Thresholds are guidelines, not universal rules. Sector differences and data quality matter.

01

Profitability

How much profit the business earns on sales and capital.

ROIC (%)

Profit after tax earned on all capital invested in the business.

NOPAT ÷ (Debt + Equity − Cash)Above roughly 12% often beats cost of capital; compare within a sector.

ROE, 5y average (%)

Profit generated per unit of shareholder equity, averaged over five years.

Net income ÷ Average equity12–15%+ can be solid. Debt can inflate ROE; read it with leverage.

Gross margin (%)

Revenue left after the direct cost of goods or services.

Gross profit ÷ RevenueHigh, stable margins may suggest pricing power. Compare within sector.

Gross margin volatility (pp)

How much gross margin moved year to year over five years.

Standard deviation of annual gross marginLower volatility can indicate more predictable margins.
02

Growth

How quickly sales and cash flow compound, and how steadily.

Revenue CAGR, 5y (%)

Annualized revenue growth over five years.

(Revenue now ÷ Revenue 5y ago)^(1/5) − 1Acquisitions can flatter growth; look for context.

FCF CAGR, 5y (%)

Annualized free-cash-flow growth over five years.

(FCF now ÷ FCF 5y ago)^(1/5) − 1Unreliable when starting free cash flow is near zero.

Revenue growth volatility (pp)

How widely annual revenue growth varies around its average.

Standard deviation of annual revenue growthCyclical businesses may score lower by design.
03

Balance sheet

Ability to withstand downturns and service debt.

Net debt / EBITDA (x)

Net debt compared with operating earnings.

(Total debt − Cash) ÷ EBITDABelow 2x is often conservative; above 4x can be stretched. Not meaningful for banks.

Interest coverage (x)

How many times operating profit covers interest.

EBIT ÷ Interest expenseAbove 5x is often comfortable; below 2x can be a warning.

Altman Z-score

A statistical composite that estimates financial distress risk.

Weighted sum of five balance-sheet and profit ratiosAbove 3 is commonly called safe; designed for manufacturers.

Piotroski F-score (0–9)

Nine pass/fail tests across profitability, leverage, and efficiency.

Count of passed tests, 0 to 98–9 is strong; 0–3 is weak in the original framework.
04

Cash flow quality

Whether reported profit turns into cash.

FCF conversion (%)

How much reported profit becomes free cash flow.

Free cash flow ÷ Net incomeNear or above 100% is often healthy; persistent weakness needs context.

Accruals ratio (% assets)

The part of profit not backed by operating cash flow.

(Net income − Operating cash flow) ÷ Average total assetsLower is generally better; high accruals may deserve a closer look.

CapEx intensity (% revenue)

Revenue reinvested in property and equipment.

Capital expenditure ÷ RevenueLower leaves more cash, but some industries need heavy investment.
05

Capital allocation

What management does with the cash the business generates.

Shareholder yield (%)

Cash returned to owners, net of new shares issued.

(Dividends + Buybacks − Issuance) ÷ Market capA negative figure means net dilution over the measured period.

Return on retained earnings (%)

Earnings growth created by profit kept in the business.

EPS change over 5y ÷ Cumulative retained EPSAbove 10% can indicate productive reinvestment; examine the period.
06

Moat

Signals related to the durability of a competitive advantage.

Market share trend (pp, 5y)

Change in estimated market share over five years.

Share now − Share 5y agoPositive means share gained. Sources may be manual or estimated.

Top-10 customer concentration (%)

Revenue dependent on the ten largest customers.

Top-10 customer revenue ÷ Total revenueHigher concentration can increase customer dependency.

R&D / revenue (%)

Share of revenue invested in research and development.

R&D expense ÷ RevenueRelevant in some sectors; switch off where it does not apply.
07

Earnings integrity

Accounting and governance signals that need careful interpretation.

Beneish M-score

A statistical model that flags patterns associated with earnings manipulation.

Eight-variable Beneish modelAbove −1.78 flags possible manipulation; it is not proof of wrongdoing.

Insider ownership (%)

Share of the company owned by executives and directors.

Insider shares ÷ Shares outstandingAlignment can help; very high ownership may also concentrate control.

Short interest (% float)

Share of tradable stock currently sold short.

Shares sold short ÷ FloatHigh short interest is market positioning, not a verdict on quality.
OPTIONAL / PROFILE CONTROL

Price context stays separate.

These multiples are never included in the company Quality Score.

P/E

Price per unit of trailing earnings.

Price ÷ EPS (trailing 12 months)Missing when earnings are negative; compare within sector.

Forward P/E

Price per unit of forecast earnings.

Price ÷ EPS (next 12 months)Depends on analyst forecasts, which can be optimistic.

EV / EBITDA

Enterprise value relative to operating earnings.

Enterprise value ÷ EBITDAIncludes debt and cash in enterprise value.

P / FCF

Market value per unit of free cash flow.

Market cap ÷ Free cash flowOne-off cash movements can distort a year.

PEG

P/E adjusted for expected earnings growth.

P/E ÷ EPS growth rate (%)Unreliable when growth is erratic.

EV / invested capital

Enterprise value relative to capital invested.

Enterprise value ÷ Invested capitalRead with ROIC; a high ratio needs context.

How a fund score is composed.

Holdings quality is weighted by position size. Past return is historical and is not a forecast.

LIMITS MATTER

What the score is not.

It does not predict prices or recommend an investment. Peer rankings depend on sector definitions, data coverage, and correct inputs. Some metrics are unsuitable for some industries. All data currently shown on this site is fictional.
